Self-pierce riveting documentation

Self-pierce riveting was my biggest documentation project at Atlas Copco. I spent two years writing guides for their SPR technology, which is how car manufacturers join aluminum body panels without welding. This is especially important for electric vehicles where every pound matters for battery range.

Why this was challenging​

Self-pierce riveting sounds simple - you push a rivet through multiple pieces of metal. But the reality is much more complex:

Process precision. The rivet geometry, material properties, and forming forces all have to be exactly right. Material science. Different aluminum alloys and steel grades behave completely differently during riveting. Quality demands. Car body joints have to last 15+ years and survive crash tests. Production reality. These systems run 24/7 in car factories where downtime costs thousands per minute.

Documentation I created​

Process fundamentals guide​

Developed comprehensive technical documentation explaining:

  • Rivet formation mechanics. How the rivet pierces the top sheet and flares in the bottom sheet.
  • Material compatibility matrices. Which material combinations work best with different rivet types.
  • Joint strength characteristics. Tensile, shear, and fatigue properties of SPR joints.
  • Quality assessment methods. Visual inspection, cross-sectioning, and automated monitoring.

Equipment selection and setup documentation​

Created detailed guides for:

  • SPR system specification. Matching equipment capabilities to production requirements.
  • Tooling selection. Dies and punches for different material combinations and thicknesses.
  • Robot integration. Programming industrial robots for consistent rivet placement.
  • Quality monitoring setup. Force/displacement monitoring for real-time quality control.

Production implementation guides​

Comprehensive documentation covering:

  • Line integration procedures. Installing SPR equipment in existing automotive production lines.
  • Process parameter optimization. Setting force, speed, and position parameters for different applications.
  • Operator training materials. Safe operation procedures and quality checkpoints.
  • Maintenance protocols. Preventive maintenance schedules and tooling replacement procedures.

Real-world applications​

Automotive body-in-white manufacturing​

SPR technology is particularly valuable for:

  • Multi-material joining. Connecting aluminum space frames to steel reinforcements.
  • Dissimilar thickness materials. Joining structural components of varying thickness.
  • Access-limited areas. Creating joints where traditional welding isn't feasible.
  • Coated materials. Joining pre-painted or galvanized materials without damaging coatings.

Electric vehicle manufacturing​

Special considerations for EV applications:

  • Lightweight construction. Maximizing range through weight reduction.
  • Battery pack integration. Secure mounting points that maintain structural integrity.
  • Corrosion resistance. Preventing galvanic corrosion in dissimilar metal joints.
  • Crash performance. Maintaining joint integrity during impact scenarios.

Technical writing approach​

Process-oriented documentation structure​

Instead of feature-based documentation, I organized content around manufacturing workflows:

  • Pre-production setup. Equipment installation and calibration procedures.
  • Production operation. Daily startup, operation, and quality monitoring procedures.
  • Quality troubleshooting. Systematic approaches to diagnosing and correcting joint quality issues.
  • Maintenance workflows. Scheduled maintenance and emergency repair procedures.

Visual documentation strategy​

SPR processes are highly visual, requiring extensive illustration:

  • Cross-section diagrams. Showing rivet formation stages and final joint geometry.
  • Force/displacement curves. Illustrating proper rivet setting characteristics.
  • Quality assessment photos. Examples of acceptable and defective joints.
  • Equipment setup diagrams. Proper positioning and alignment procedures.

Business impact through documentation​

Manufacturing efficiency improvements​

Well-documented SPR procedures directly improved production metrics:

  • Setup time reduction. Standardized procedures reduced equipment changeover time by 30%.
  • Quality consistency. Clear parameter specifications reduced joint quality variation by 25%.
  • Operator training time. Structured training materials reduced certification time from 3 weeks to 10 days.
  • Troubleshooting speed. Systematic diagnostic procedures reduced downtime by 40%.

Global standardization benefits​

Documentation enabled consistent SPR implementation across Atlas Copco's international customers:

  • Process standardization. Same procedures and quality standards regardless of manufacturing location.
  • Knowledge transfer. Rapid deployment of SPR technology to new production facilities.
  • Supplier qualification. Clear requirements for rivet suppliers and quality standards.
  • Regulatory compliance. Documentation supporting automotive quality certifications (IATF 16949, VDA).

Advanced SPR applications​

Multi-stack joining​

Documentation for complex joining scenarios:

  • Three+ material stacks. Procedures for joining multiple materials simultaneously.
  • Hybrid assemblies. Combining SPR with welding, bonding, or clinching.
  • Structural optimization. Joint placement for optimal load distribution.
  • Fatigue performance. Long-term durability in cyclic loading applications.

Process monitoring and Industry 4.0 integration​

Advanced documentation covering:

  • Real-time quality monitoring. Force signature analysis for immediate quality feedback.
  • Data collection systems. Integration with MES and quality management systems.
  • Predictive maintenance. Using process data to predict tooling wear and replacement needs.
  • Traceability systems. Complete joint history tracking for automotive recall prevention.

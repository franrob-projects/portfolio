# My work at Atlas Copco

I worked as a Technical Writer at Atlas Copco from 2018 to 2021, writing documentation for industrial manufacturing equipment. My job was translating complex joining and fastening technologies into guides that engineers and factory workers could actually use.


## About Atlas Copco

Atlas Copco is a global industrial equipment company. I worked specifically with their [Industrial Technique Business Area (ITBA)](https://www.atlascopco.com/en-er/itba), the division that develops joining and fastening solutions for automotive, aerospace, and heavy industrial manufacturers.


## What I actually worked on

### Self-pierce riveting documentation
This was my biggest project - documenting Atlas Copco's [self-pierce riveting technology](https://www.atlascopco.com/en-er/itba/products/joining-solutions/self-pierce-riveting). Self-pierce riveting is how they join aluminum car body panels without welding. I wrote the equipment manuals, process guides, and troubleshooting documentation that factory technicians use daily.


### Assembly line documentation  
I documented the robotic systems that install rivets and fasteners on automotive production lines. This meant writing installation guides, operation procedures, and maintenance schedules for million-dollar factory equipment.

### Training materials
Created the training guides that Atlas Copco uses to teach customers how to use their equipment. These materials needed to work for everyone from PhD engineers to factory floor technicians.

## Why manufacturing documentation is different

Writing for manufacturing is nothing like writing software docs. Here's what makes it challenging:

**Safety comes first.** One mistake in the documentation could seriously hurt someone. Every procedure had to include safety warnings and lockout/tagout procedures.

**Multiple audiences.** The same piece of equipment gets used by design engineers, maintenance techs, and machine operators. They all need different information from the same system.

**Global deployment.** My documentation went to factories in Germany, China, Mexico, and everywhere else Atlas Copco sells equipment. It had to work across languages and safety regulations.

**Complex physics.** Joining technologies involve materials science, mechanical engineering, and process control. Making this accessible without dumbing it down was the constant challenge.


## My approach to industrial documentation

### Task-based writing
Instead of describing features, I organized everything around what people actually need to do:
- Getting equipment installed and running
- Daily operation procedures  
- Preventive maintenance schedules
- Fixing things when they break

### Safety integration
Rather than putting all safety info in one section that nobody reads, I embedded safety warnings exactly where people need them in each procedure.


## Major joining technology projects

### Self-pierce riveting systems
I documented how this technology works and when to use it. Self-pierce riveting is crucial for lightweight car construction, especially electric vehicles where every pound matters for battery range.


### Automated joining system documentation
Wrote guides for integrating Atlas Copco joining equipment into existing factory automation. This covered robot programming for riveting and fastening operations, plus quality monitoring systems for joint integrity.

## What I learned

Working at Atlas Copco taught me that technical writing isn't just about explaining how things work - it's about enabling people to do their jobs safely and efficiently. When your documentation goes to factories around the world, you realize how important clear, practical writing really is.

The manufacturing industry showed me documentation at its most critical - where unclear instructions don't just frustrate users, they can shut down production lines or hurt people. That experience shaped how I approach all technical writing now.
